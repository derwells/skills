# Mode: Curate


`/learn curate` — no lesson taught. This is course maintenance done *with* the user, and it has historically been the highest-leverage session type: run one rather than letting friction accrete. Suggest it yourself when the same friction shows up across two or three sessions, when the contract has grown past ~a dozen rules, or when the user sounds annoyed at the course rather than at a concept.

1. **Diagnose from evidence.** Read the actual recent conversation when available, alongside logs, progress, and teaching materials. Tutor-written summaries may miss the repeated learner questions. Check whether definitions and mathematics arrived before the learner needed them, whether the tutor's claims were accurate, and whether examples left room to think. Distinguish these failures from a scope problem or unfamiliar code. A lesson taking several sessions is not itself a failure. Check whether older contract rules conflict with the learner's latest request.
2. **Resolve the actual decision.** Use preferences the learner already stated. Ask only if goals, depth, participation, or scope remain unclear. A request for professor-led teaching does not authorize discarding a chosen subject or converting a listening preference into mandatory drills.
3. **Repair the lesson design.** Rewrite the upcoming lesson around a question and an argument. Prepare the mathematical or conceptual foundation, a checked example, and a deliberate place to stop. Keep contrast explanations available to the tutor without requiring their immediate delivery. Use [lesson-craft.md](lesson-craft.md). Adjust the curriculum only where its sequence or goals caused the problem. Change the materials as well as the contract so the next session has something concrete to teach. For code friction, read [code-reading.md](code-reading.md), unpack the current example, and place operations beside their mathematical meaning. Preserve conceptual progress and distinguish tutor mistakes, syntax support, and conceptual remediation.
4. **Compact and record.** Keep the contract short; preserve explicit learner choices and retire superseded tutor-generated restrictions. Update learner-level preferences in `learner.md` and course-specific decisions in the course. Use `references/progress-schema.md` for evidence judgments and legacy `shaky` entries. Validate progress JSON. Record a dated maintenance entry; do not count curation as teaching, increment teaching hours, or fabricate learner evidence.
